# Groundwork

### From idea to approved.

## Vision

Groundwork helps anyone understand **what they need to do before they can legally start a real-world project**.

Opening a café, renovating a home, starting a daycare, adding outdoor seating, converting a garage, opening a salon, or changing how a property is used can involve permits, licences, inspections, registrations, forms, and approvals spread across multiple agencies and government websites.

The information usually exists. The problem is that it is fragmented, difficult to interpret, location-specific, and full of dependencies.

Groundwork turns that complexity into a clear, source-backed plan.

---

## The problem

Today, someone starting a project has to figure out:

* Which government bodies have jurisdiction
* Which permits and licences apply to their specific project
* What forms and documents are required
* Which approvals depend on others
* What inspections are needed
* How long each step may take
* Whether requirements have changed
* What incoming government emails mean for the project

The result is usually dozens of tabs, PDFs, phone calls, spreadsheets, consultants, and uncertainty.

Missing one requirement can mean delays, fines, failed inspections, or having to redo work.

---

## The product

Groundwork begins with a conversation.

The user describes what they want to do:

> “I’m opening a 35-seat café in South Delhi.”

Groundwork asks for the minimum information it needs to begin, such as the project type and location.

Then it starts researching.

As Groundwork discovers relevant authorities, regulations and requirements, the research itself can reveal questions that need answering.

For example:

> Will food be prepared on-site?

> Are you modifying the building’s ventilation?

> Will there be outdoor seating?

> Will alcohol be served?

> Is the property already approved for this type of use?

These questions are not a fixed onboarding form.

**Groundwork researches, learns what matters, asks when necessary, and continues researching with the new information.**

The experience is an iterative loop:

**Describe → Research → Clarify → Research deeper → Build the plan**

The user should never need to understand the regulatory system well enough to know what information Groundwork needs.

Groundwork discovers that along the way.

---

## The project workspace

As research progresses, Groundwork builds a project-specific workspace containing:

**Checklist**
Every confirmed permit, licence, registration, filing, inspection, and prerequisite.

**Timeline**
The order things should happen, dependencies between approvals, and the likely critical path.

**Requirements**
Forms, documents, fees, eligibility rules, inspections, official links, and supporting evidence.

**Coverage**
What Groundwork has confirmed, what does not apply, and what still needs verification.

The workspace can begin forming before the research is finished.

A requirement may initially appear as:

**Fire approval · Checking applicability…**

and later become:

**Fire approval · Required**

or:

**Fire approval · Not applicable**

The product should feel like Groundwork is actively investigating the project rather than generating a static report.

The goal is not to give users a pile of government links.

The goal is to answer:

> **What exactly stands between where I am now and being allowed to start?**

---

## A living project, not a one-time report

Every Groundwork project receives its own AI-powered inbox.

Users can forward government correspondence, inspection notices, approval emails, forms, contractor documents, and attachments to it.

Groundwork understands what changed and updates the project automatically.

An email saying:

> “Your fire inspection is scheduled for September 22. Please provide Form B and an electrical safety certificate.”

can immediately become:

**Fire inspection — September 22, 10:30 AM**

* Prepare Form B
* Upload electrical safety certificate
* Inspection scheduled

Incoming information can also trigger new research.

A government email may reference a regulation Groundwork has not seen before. Groundwork can investigate it, update the approval path, and ask the user another question if the new requirement depends on something it does not yet know.

The project evolves alongside the real-world process.

---

## Trust is part of the product

Groundwork should never pretend it knows more than it does.

Every important requirement is tied to an official source.

When sources disagree, Groundwork shows the disagreement.

When something cannot be confidently confirmed, it is marked as unresolved rather than guessed.

Groundwork should distinguish clearly between:

**Confirmed**
Supported by authoritative sources.

**Not applicable**
Checked and ruled out based on the project.

**Needs verification**
Evidence is incomplete, ambiguous, or conflicting.

Groundwork should also make its research coverage visible. It should be possible to see which jurisdictions, authorities and regulatory areas have already been checked and which are still being investigated.

The promise is not:

> “AI knows every regulation.”

The promise is:

> **“We show you what we found, why it applies, where it came from, and what still needs checking.”**

---

## Why now

Regulatory information is increasingly available online, but it remains organised around agencies rather than around what a person is trying to accomplish.

AI can now understand a user's intent, search across fragmented sources, read complex government pages and PDFs, identify dependencies, ask context-specific questions, and continuously reconcile new information.

Groundwork reorganises the regulatory web around the **project**, not the bureaucracy.

---

## Long-term vision

Groundwork becomes the starting point for doing anything in the physical world that requires permission.

Not a database of permits.

Not a search engine for regulations.

Not software for one city or one industry.

A universal layer between:

**“I want to do this.”**

and

**“You are cleared to begin.”**

A person should be able to describe a project anywhere in the world and have Groundwork progressively investigate it, ask the questions that matter, and build a trustworthy path from **idea → requirements → approvals → ready**.
